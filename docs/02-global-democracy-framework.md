# Global Democracy Framework

## Purpose
Develop voluntary, interoperable public-interest infrastructure that supports civic participation and helps institutions make democratic processes more accessible, transparent and accountable.

## Normative foundations
The framework should be assessed against:
- UN Charter;
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights, including participation in public affairs;
- International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, especially Article 25;
- applicable regional human-rights instruments;
- national constitutions, election laws and data-protection regimes.

This project is independent and does not speak for the United Nations.

## Principles
1. Human rights and democratic self-determination.
2. Subsidiarity: decisions remain at the lowest competent democratic level.
3. Political neutrality and pluralism.
4. Privacy, data minimization and purpose limitation.
5. Accessibility and inclusion, including offline channels.
6. Transparency, public observation and independent remedies.
7. Interoperability without centralized global voter databases.
8. Local legal sovereignty and accountable public institutions.

## Architecture
A federated model is preferred: local and national institutions retain authority over their own processes; shared open standards support interoperability, audit methods and accessible participation. No global identity registry, global ballot box or supranational election authority is proposed.

## Initial workstreams
- Open civic-dialogue software and participation standards.
- Digital identity interoperability research, without assuming identity equals voting eligibility.
- Privacy-preserving eligibility research in synthetic test environments.
- Public-verifiability and audit research.
- Accessibility, digital literacy and offline inclusion.
- Comparative constitutional and election-law research.

## Limits
A public consultation is not an election, referendum or representative poll unless lawfully constituted and methodologically designed as such. Open online participation is self-selected and cannot be described as the will of a population.
