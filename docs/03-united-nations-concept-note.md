# Concept Note for United Nations Engagement

## Title
**Digital Public Infrastructure for Inclusive Civic Participation: A Human-Rights-Based, Federated and Open-Source Approach**

## Proponent
WODA DEMOCRACY OS — an independent open-source research initiative. This concept note is a proposal for discussion and does not imply UN affiliation, endorsement or submission.

## Executive summary
WODA DEMOCRACY OS proposes a voluntary research and standards dialogue on how open, privacy-preserving digital public infrastructure can support civic participation while protecting human rights, democratic self-determination and national legal sovereignty.

Germany is proposed as an initial comparative case study because its constitutional framework provides a clear example of legally structured representative democracy, constitutional amendment procedures and public-verifiability requirements. The initiative does not presume that Germany should adopt internet voting or direct presidential elections. Those are separate political and constitutional choices for the German people and their institutions.

## Why this matters
Digital tools can widen access to public information, consultations and participatory processes. They can also intensify exclusion, surveillance, manipulation, coercion and cyber risk. The international community needs evidence-based principles, not a one-size-fits-all global voting platform.

## Proposed UN-facing work
1. Convene an expert dialogue on human-rights-based digital civic participation.
2. Develop a voluntary principles and safeguards framework with states, civil society, academia, disability advocates and election experts.
3. Commission comparative research on digital identity, privacy-preserving eligibility, public verifiability and remote-voting risks.
4. Support non-binding civic-dialogue pilots with transparent methodology and independent evaluation.
5. Publish open technical specifications and model procurement requirements for public-interest civic technology.
6. Encourage capacity-building so lower-resource communities are not excluded.

## Germany demonstration pathway
Germany may serve as a first case study:
- constitutional analysis of Article 54 and the Article 79 amendment process;
- open public discussion about institutional options;
- research on EUDI wallet interoperability and privacy;
- a neutral, non-binding civic-dialogue pilot;
- independent legal, security, accessibility and privacy evaluation.

A change to the German Basic Law would require the constitutional process and supermajorities prescribed by Article 79(2), subject to Article 79(3). A WODA pilot cannot amend the Basic Law or conduct an official election.

## Safeguards
- No global voter register or centralized global identity database.
- No claim that a wallet login establishes voting eligibility.
- No binding online election pilot without a clear legal basis and competent election authority.
- No candidate or party control of the project.
- Independent review, public reporting, accessible offline alternatives and meaningful remedies.
- Strict separation of identity, eligibility, ballot and audit functions.

## Requested engagement
We invite relevant UN entities, Member States, election-management bodies, civil society and academic institutions to consider an exploratory, multi-stakeholder dialogue and provide guidance on appropriate existing forums and processes.

## Suggested initial interlocutors
Potentially relevant bodies and forums include the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs (UNDESA), the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), UNDP electoral assistance and digital-governance programmes, and relevant ECOSOC civil-society participation mechanisms. The appropriate route and eligibility must be confirmed directly with each institution.

## Proposed 12-month outputs
- Comparative legal and human-rights mapping.
- Voluntary principles draft.
- Open-source civic-dialogue prototype.
- Independent pilot evaluation.
- Public report on opportunities, risks and unresolved questions.
