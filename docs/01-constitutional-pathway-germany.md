# Germany: constitutional and legislative pathway

**Discussion paper only — not legal advice, a bill, or an initiated parliamentary procedure.**

## Current constitutional position
Article 54 of the German Basic Law provides for election of the Federal President by the Federal Convention (Bundesversammlung). A direct popular election cannot be introduced by software or ordinary administrative action; it requires constitutional amendment.

Article 79(2) requires approval by two thirds of the Members of the Bundestag and two thirds of the votes of the Bundesrat. Article 79(3) protects, among other things, the democratic principles in Articles 1 and 20. Any reform must preserve the constitutional order and the parliamentary system.

## Proposed process
1. Commission independent opinions from constitutional, electoral and administrative-law experts.
2. Publish alternatives: retain the Federal Convention, introduce direct election, or test other models.
3. Consult Länder, parties, civil society, election administrators, disability organizations and data-protection authorities.
4. Draft a complete amendment addressing Articles 54–56 and related provisions, transitional rules and presidential functions.
5. Draft a Federal Presidential Election Act covering nominations, campaign finance, rounds, administration, scrutiny, remedies, accessibility and continuity.
6. Conduct parliamentary deliberation and, if supported, the Article 79(2) process.
7. Keep any digital voting channel separate from the constitutional question and subject it to its own evidence-based legal and security assessment.

## Digital election safeguards
A digital channel must not be presumed safe because it uses an EUDI wallet or blockchain. Identity proof is not proof of electoral eligibility; eligibility proof must not reveal ballot choice. Remote voting also raises coercion, malware, secrecy, availability and public-verifiability concerns.

Any future system must preserve equal access, secrecy, freedom of the vote, independent scrutiny, recount and judicial remedies, and practical non-digital participation. The Federal Constitutional Court's 2009 voting-computer judgment is a central reference for public verifiability of essential election steps.

## Germany as a learning case, not an exportable template
Germany can contribute a transparent constitutional debate, rigorous privacy engineering, open standards and independently evaluated pilots. Other states must choose their own constitutional and institutional arrangements through their own democratic processes.
